# Two other ways to use the same method

The Tokyo example covers the full workflow. These shorter examples show what changes when the AI works on your computer or helps you decide what fits into a week.

*Both examples are fictional illustrations. No personal files were accessed and no tasks were scheduled.*

## Organize a practice copy of Downloads

Start with: “Help me make Downloads easier to browse. Inspect the candidate folder I select, ask about useful categories, and propose a copied practice folder. Keep my originals.”

You select a candidate containing 12 files across several subfolders and allow inspection. The agent reads the folder, checks access, and asks:

> “Should work documents stay together, or be grouped by file type? Should I rename files? Where should unclear items go? May I inspect document contents to help classify them?”

You choose purpose-based folders, permit content inspection, prefer existing filenames, and ask for a Review folder when the purpose is unclear. Age and size can help describe the clutter; neither establishes that something is disposable.

The agent proposes:

> “I'll copy these 12 files into Downloads Practice. I'll try six representative files, including an unclear document and two receipts with the same filename. I'll avoid overwriting anything and record every old and new location. I'll check the file count, compare file contents with the originals, and confirm the move record accounts for the changes. After those checks, I'll show you the trial and wait for approval before organizing the other six.”

You approve. With file tools available, the agent creates the copy and folders. Before moving the six trial files, it finds `Email attachments/receipt.pdf` and `Scans/receipt.pdf` would both become `Receipts/receipt.pdf`. It proposes retaining one name and naming the other `receipt-email-attachments.pdf`, then waits for your choice. After you agree, it makes the moves. `scan.pdf` stays unchanged in Review because its purpose is uncertain.

The checked trial shows you actual folders and the move record. You approve the arrangement. The agent finishes and reports:

> “Downloads Practice contains all 12 files: five in Receipts, three in Work, two in Installers, and two in Review. Every file matches its original contents. The move record covers all 12 files; no destination was overwritten. Your original folder is intact. Review contains `scan.pdf` and `final-final.pdf`, which still need your decisions.”

You now have a browsable result to judge, with a record that makes the changes traceable.

## Turn an overloaded week into a doable plan

Your brain dump says: “Client deck due Thursday noon. Dentist Tuesday 3 p.m. Laundry. Renew passport sometime. Call Mum. Friday application deadline. I'm behind on everything.”

The AI asks about priorities, deadlines, remaining effort, and available time. You answer: the deck comes first and needs four hours; the application needs two hours; both deadlines are fixed; Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday each have two free hours. The dentist is already booked. The passport can wait.

It proposes a short plan, checks the six-hour limit and deadline order, and waits for approval. After you approve, the finished plan is:

> **Monday:** deck outline and rough slides, two hours. Start laundry during a break.
>
> **Tuesday:** keep the dentist appointment. Call Mum afterward if you have energy.
>
> **Wednesday:** finish and check the deck, two hours; ready before Thursday noon.
>
> **Thursday:** complete and submit the application, two hours; ready before Friday's deadline.

Passport renewal moves to next week's list. The plan stays in the conversation. Calendar entries and recurring jobs would need a separate request.
