# Plan a Tokyo trip around what you actually like

*A fictional conversation and shortened itinerary to illustrate the method. This is not a researched travel recommendation.*

Your flights and hotel are booked. You have four days in Tokyo and a few saved links. “Plan my trip” leaves the AI to decide what a good holiday means. A few useful questions can give it a much better brief.

## Start with what you know

> Help me plan four days in Tokyo around food, coffee, shopping, and time to wander. Here are my dates, hotel, and saved links.
>
> Research Tabelog, r/JapanTravel, Japan Guide, and Time Out Tokyo. First interview me about my preferences, including how to use my saved links and whether to include other sources. Explain unfamiliar choices and help me decide.
>
> Show me a brief plan, including how you'll check the itinerary, and wait for my go-ahead. Then research and build one day, check it, and show me. Wait for my approval before doing the other three.

Paste your details and links below the request. You don't need to work out every preference before starting; discovering them is part of the conversation.

## Answer the questions that shape the trip

The AI reads what you supplied, then asks about the missing decisions. Here is part of that exchange:

**AI:** “How do you like spending a day? Any limits on walking or queues?”

**You:** “Start around 11. One main area each day, with room to wander. Walking is fine with café breaks. No queues longer than about 20 minutes.”

**AI:** “What kind of food and shopping? Any dietary restrictions or spending limits?”

**You:** “No dietary restrictions. Vintage clothes, small design shops, and good coffee. Up to ¥10,000 per person a day for food, except one special dinner where I'd go up to ¥20,000.”

**AI:** “Which saved places are must-dos? Are the named websites your priorities or the only sources? May I also check Japanese-language pages and venue and transport websites?”

**You:** “The vintage shops in Shimokitazawa are must-dos. The ramen link is just an idea. Those sites are priorities; the extra sources are fine.”

The saved ramen place now has to earn its place in the itinerary. A long queue could rule it out. The vintage shops have to fit, and the AI should ask before dropping them.

## Approve the approach and try one day

The AI's recap could be:

> I'll group your must-dos by area, use [Tabelog](https://tabelog.com/en/help/beginner) for restaurant candidates, [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/JapanTravel/) for traveler experiences, and [Japan Guide](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2164.html) and [Time Out Tokyo](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo) for neighborhood and shopping ideas. I'll use venue and transport pages to check practical details.
>
> I'll check opening days, estimated travel and food costs, and booking requirements. I'll flag unknowns, including queue lengths I can't predict. First I'll show you one relaxed day with an optional afternoon. Shall I start?

You approve. The AI researches, builds the first day, and checks it before showing you. You review whether it feels like your trip, then approve expansion or request a specific change.

For Reddit, ask it to read the accessible comments and replies in selected threads and report any coverage gaps. Repeated opinions are useful leads, not verified facts.

## See your answers in the result

After you approve the checked first day, the AI completes the four-day itinerary. Here is an excerpt from that finished result. The venue names and lunch allowance below are fictional; the times illustrate your chosen pace.

> **Day 1 · Shimokitazawa**
>
> **11:00 — Coffee and your saved shops.** Have coffee near the station, then browse Elm Vintage and North Street Vintage, the two shops you marked as must-dos. There is no next booking to rush toward.
>
> **13:00 — Lunch.** Try Corner Noodles, with a planning allowance of ¥2,500 per person. If the queue is over 20 minutes, switch to the backup, Station Kitchen. Your saved ramen place remains an optional swap.
>
> **14:30 onward — Leave it open.** Wander, take another café break, or head back whenever you like. An extra shop is optional. Your special dinner is on day four, so there is no reservation to plan this afternoon around.

A researched version would link each stop to its supporting pages and include travel estimates, costs, booking tasks, and remaining uncertainties. This illustration contains no checked venues, routes, or availability.
